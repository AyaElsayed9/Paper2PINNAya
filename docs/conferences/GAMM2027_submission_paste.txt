===== TITLE =====
Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels

===== ABSTRACT =====
The design of omega-stringer-stiffened composite panels is often governed by local skin buckling, so design studies need the critical load of many variants. Shell finite element models are accurate but expensive, and data-driven surrogates need large labelled datasets and cannot estimate the error of a single prediction. Our previous hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN) used a plate energy with a reduced bending stiffness and recomputed doubtful predictions with Abaqus.

Here, the PINN is built entirely on Generalized Beam Theory (GBT), which replaces both the plate energy and Abaqus. GBT describes the cross-section deformation by a few cross-section modes, each multiplied by an amplitude function along the length; our formulation for periodic panel cells with laminated walls agrees with Abaqus within 1.6 % in 60 validation cases.

The method has three steps. First, all designs are described in one common reduced basis, so that one network can cover many designs: cross-section modes from a proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) of the GBT buckling modes of 64 sample panels, and longitudinal functions from a fixed set of sines and end-zone splines. This basis changes the critical load by at most 0.3 %. Second, a PINN for each lay-up predicts, from the stringer pitch and the panel aspect ratio, the critical load and eight candidate buckling modes; a decoder generates the cross-section shape for each longitudinal function, so that the buckle pattern can vary with the design. The network is trained on 152 designs by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient of its modes, with physics only or with up to 120 GBT results. Training succeeded only after scaling the coordinates by the strain energy and combining each training step with one GBT eigenvalue iteration per design. Third, every prediction is checked: the predicted modes start a block eigenvalue solver (LOBPCG) with the GBT matrices of the design. Each iteration gives an upper bound of the critical load in the reduced basis, and the relative residual gives an error estimate without a reference solution. The solver stops when this estimate is below the tolerance; if this is not reached after 40 iterations, full GBT is used instead.

A single network for all lay-ups, like standard data-driven models, kept maximum errors above 20 %. For four lay-ups and two lateral boundary conditions (320 unseen designs), the networks trained with 120 GBT results predicted the critical load within 4.9 %; physics-only networks overestimated the load, but their modes were still good starting vectors. The error estimate was never smaller than the true error in 3800 checks. For a 5 % tolerance, the solver needed 3 to 15 iterations and no design required full GBT; without GBT results, the maximum error was 2.5 % at 7 to 8 s per design instead of 11 s. Gaussian process regression needed 80 GBT results per lay-up and gave no guarantee. At the 22 Abaqus points of the previous study, the new PINN with the check deviated from Abaqus by at most 1.19 %, against 10.80 % for the previous PINN.
