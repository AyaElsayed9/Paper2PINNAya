Title: Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels
Section: S25 Machine Learning and Data Science in Applied Mathematics and Mechanics (alternative: S04 Structural Mechanics)
Keywords: physics-informed neural networks; Generalized Beam Theory; buckling; stiffened composite panels; a posteriori error estimate; reduced-order model

Buckling governs the design of thin-walled stiffened composite panels, and design studies need the critical load of many panel variants. Finite element models are accurate but expensive, and data-driven surrogates need many labelled analyses while giving no information on the error of a single prediction. In earlier work, we proposed a hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN) for omega-stringer panels whose physics rested on a reduced bending stiffness approximation of the classical plate energy and whose oracle was a finite element model.

Here, the physics is replaced by a shear-deformable Generalized Beam Theory (GBT) formulation of a periodic panel cell with laminated walls and the true stringer offset, in which the periodic boundary conditions are built into the cross-section analysis. A shared reduced mode basis and a dictionary of longitudinal functions give the stiffness and geometric operators of every design in one common space. Lay-up-specific networks map the stringer pitch and the panel aspect ratio to candidate buckling modes and the critical load. They are trained by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient of the GBT energy, with an energy-whitened natural-gradient step, either from physics alone or with a few GBT labels.

The predicted modes start a block LOBPCG iteration in the reduced GBT space of the design. Every iterate is an upper bound of the critical load, and the relative residual of the iterate gives an error estimate that needs no labels. The iteration stops when this estimate falls below a chosen tolerance.

GBT agrees with Abaqus within 0.54 % on average at the validation points of the earlier study, compared with 2.53 % for the earlier PINN. For four lay-ups and two lateral conditions (320 unseen designs), the network trained with 120 labels predicts the critical load instantly with mean errors of 0.24 to 1.18 %. Without labels, its prediction is biased high, but the learned modes are good starting vectors. The error estimate was never smaller than the true error in 3800 checks, and its rank correlation with the true error reaches 0.88. A 5 % tolerance is met on all designs without any labels in about 7 s per design, against 11 s for a full GBT solution. A Gaussian process regression on the same inputs needs at least 80 labels to reach this accuracy and provides neither a guarantee nor the mode shapes.
