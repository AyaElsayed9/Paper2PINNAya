Title: Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels
Section: S25 Machine Learning and Data Science in Applied Mathematics and Mechanics (alternative: S04 Structural Mechanics)
Keywords: physics-informed neural networks; Generalized Beam Theory; buckling; stiffened composite panels; a posteriori error estimate; reduced-order model

Local skin buckling governs the design of omega-stringer-stiffened composite panels, and design studies require the critical load of many variants. Shell finite element (FE) models are accurate but costly, whereas data-driven surrogates require large labelled datasets and provide no error bound for individual predictions. In previous work, we proposed a hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN) based on a reduced-bending-stiffness plate energy, with Abaqus as the oracle for flagged predictions.

Here, the PINN is built entirely on a shear-deformable Generalized Beam Theory (GBT) formulation with periodic boundary conditions, which replaces both the plate energy and the FE oracle. GBT represents the cross-section deformation by a small set of deformation modes with longitudinal amplitude functions; for periodic panel cells with laminated walls, it agrees with Abaqus within 1.6 % in 60 validation cases.

The approach comprises three steps. First, all designs are expressed in a common reduced basis: an energy-weighted proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) of the lowest GBT buckling modes of 64 sample panels on a fixed nodal layout provides the cross-section basis, and a dictionary of fixed-wavelength harmonics and end-zone splines provides the longitudinal basis. Assembling the elastic and geometric stiffness matrices of each design in this basis changes the critical load by at most 0.3 %. Second, a lay-up-specific PINN maps the stringer pitch and the panel aspect ratio to an instant critical load and eight candidate buckling modes, using a design encoder and a function decoder that generates the cross-section coefficients of each longitudinal basis function. It is trained on 152 designs by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient of its modes, physics-only or with up to 120 GBT labels (hybrid loss). Training converged only with energy-whitened coordinates (condition numbers of 10^9 to 10^19 otherwise) and natural-gradient updates, each starting with one GBT eigen-iteration per design. Third, each prediction is certified: its modes initialise a block LOBPCG iteration for the generalized eigenvalue problem of the design. Every iterate yields an upper bound of the reduced-model critical load, and the relative residual provides a label-free a posteriori error estimate. The iteration stops once this estimate falls below the tolerance; otherwise, after 40 iterations, full GBT is used.

A single network across arbitrary lay-ups and standard data-driven regressors both retained maximum errors above 20 %. For four lay-ups and two lateral boundary conditions (320 out-of-sample designs), the hybrid PINNs (120 labels) predicted the critical load within 4.9 %; physics-only PINNs overestimated the load but still provided effective initial subspaces. The error estimate never underestimated the true error in 3800 checks. For a 5 % tolerance, 3 to 15 iterations sufficed and no fallback to full GBT occurred; without labels, the maximum error was 2.5 % at 7 to 8 s per design, versus 11 s for full GBT. Gaussian process regression required 80 labels per lay-up for the same tolerance, without a guarantee. At the 22 Abaqus reference points of the previous study, the certified PINN deviated from Abaqus by at most 1.19 %, versus 10.80 % for the previous PINN.
