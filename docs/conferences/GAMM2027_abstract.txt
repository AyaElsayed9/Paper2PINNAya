Title: Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels
Section: S25 Machine Learning and Data Science in Applied Mathematics and Mechanics (alternative: S04 Structural Mechanics)
Keywords: physics-informed neural networks; Generalized Beam Theory; buckling; stiffened composite panels; a posteriori error estimate; reduced-order model

Design studies of thin-walled stiffened composite panels need the buckling load of many variants. Finite element models are expensive, and data-driven surrogates need many labelled analyses and give no error information for a single prediction. Our earlier hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN) for omega-stringer panels used a reduced bending stiffness approximation of the plate energy and a finite element model as oracle.

Here, the physics is replaced by a shear-deformable Generalized Beam Theory (GBT) formulation of a periodic panel cell with laminated walls, the true stringer offset and periodic boundary conditions built into the cross-section analysis. A shared reduced mode basis and a dictionary of longitudinal functions give the stiffness and geometric operators of every design in one common space. Lay-up-specific networks map the stringer pitch and the panel aspect ratio to candidate buckling modes and the critical load. They are trained by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient of the GBT energy, with an energy-whitened natural-gradient step, either from physics alone or with up to 120 GBT labels per lay-up.

The predicted modes start a block LOBPCG iteration in this reduced space. Every iterate is an upper bound of the critical load of the reduced model, which agrees with full GBT within 0.3 % in our tests, and the relative residual of the iterate gives an error estimate that needs no labels. The iteration stops when this estimate falls below a chosen tolerance.

At the validation points of the earlier study, GBT deviates from Abaqus by 0.54 % on average (earlier PINN: 2.53 %). For four lay-ups and two lateral conditions (320 unseen designs), the network trained with 120 labels predicts the critical load instantly with mean errors of 0.24 to 1.18 %. Without labels, its prediction is biased high; as starting vectors, its modes save about two iterations over a random start, as many as the modes of the nearest labelled design. The error estimate was never smaller than the true error in 3800 checks; its rank correlation with the true error reaches 0.88. A 5 % tolerance is met on all designs without any labels, even from a random start, in about 7 to 8 s per design, against 11 s for a full GBT solution. A Gaussian process regression on the same inputs needed 80 labels per lay-up to reach this accuracy (40 were not enough) and provides neither a guarantee nor the mode shapes.
