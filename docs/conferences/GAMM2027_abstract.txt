Title: Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels
Section: S25 Machine Learning and Data Science in Applied Mathematics and Mechanics (alternative: S04 Structural Mechanics)
Keywords: physics-informed neural networks; Generalized Beam Theory; buckling; stiffened composite panels; a posteriori error estimate; reduced-order model

Local skin buckling often governs the design of omega-stringer-stiffened composite panels, so design studies need the critical load of many variants. Finite element models are expensive, and data-driven models cannot estimate the error of a single prediction. Our previous hybrid physics-informed neural network (PINN) used a plate energy with a reduced bending stiffness and recomputed doubtful predictions with Abaqus.

Here, we build the PINN entirely on Generalized Beam Theory (GBT), which replaces both the plate energy and Abaqus. GBT describes the cross-section deformation by a few modes, each multiplied by an amplitude function along the length. Our formulation for periodic cells with laminated walls deviates from Abaqus by at most 1.52 % in 60 validation cases.

First, we describe all designs in one reduced basis, so networks can cover many designs. The cross-section modes come from a proper orthogonal decomposition of the GBT buckling modes of 64 sample panels. Along the length, we use sine waves with fixed wavelengths plus local spline functions near the loaded ends. In 1064 designs, this basis changed the critical load by at most 0.49 %. Second, we train separate PINNs for each of four lay-ups and two lateral boundary conditions. Each maps the stringer pitch and the aspect ratio to the critical load and eight candidate buckling modes. An encoder (four layers of 256 neurons) processes the inputs, and a decoder (two layers of 256 neurons) builds the cross-section shape for each longitudinal function. For each case, we train two networks on 152 designs by minimizing the Rayleigh quotient of their modes: one with physics only, and one that also uses full GBT solutions of 120 of the 152 designs. Training only worked after two changes: we scaled the coordinates by the strain energy, and in each training round we improved the predicted modes by one GBT eigenvalue iteration per design and fitted the network to them. Third, we check every prediction. The predicted modes start a block eigenvalue solver (LOBPCG) with the design's GBT matrices. Each iteration gives an upper bound of the critical load in this basis, and the relative residual gives an error estimate without a reference solution. The solver stops when this estimate is below the tolerance; otherwise, full GBT is used after 40 iterations.

We tested 320 unseen designs. With 120 GBT solutions, the networks predicted the critical load within 4.92 %. The physics-only networks overestimated the load in 319 of 320 designs but gave good starting modes. After 0 to 12 iterations, the error estimate was never smaller than the actual error against full GBT (3800 comparisons). For a 5 % tolerance, the solver needed 3 to 15 iterations, and no design needed full GBT. Without GBT solutions, the maximum error was 2.51 %, at 7.4 s per design on average, versus 10.7 s for full GBT. At the 22 Abaqus points of the previous study, the checked result deviated from Abaqus by at most 1.19 %, against 10.80 % for the previous PINN at the same points.
