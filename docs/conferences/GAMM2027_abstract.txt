Title: Physics-informed neural networks with a certified Generalized Beam Theory check for the buckling of stiffened composite panels
Section: S25 Machine Learning and Data Science in Applied Mathematics and Mechanics (alternative: S04 Structural Mechanics)
Keywords: physics-informed neural networks; Generalized Beam Theory; buckling; stiffened composite panels; a posteriori error estimate; reduced-order model

Composite panels stiffened by omega-shaped stringers are common in aircraft, and their design is often governed by local buckling of the skin between the stringers. A design study therefore needs the critical buckling load of many panel variants. Shell finite element models are accurate but expensive, and purely data-driven models need many training results and cannot tell how wrong a prediction is. In an earlier study, we used a physics-informed neural network (PINN) whose physics was a simplified plate model and whose doubtful predictions were recomputed with Abaqus.

Here we present a new PINN in which both roles are taken by Generalized Beam Theory (GBT). GBT describes the deformation of the whole stringer cross-section by a few cross-section modes, each multiplied by a function along the panel length. Our GBT formulation models one periodic cell of the panel with laminated walls and agrees with Abaqus within 1.6 % in 60 periodic test cases.

The method has three steps. First, every design is written in the same unknowns: 241 cross-section shapes, extracted from the GBT modes of 64 panels, combined with a fixed set of sine waves and end functions along the length. This reproduces the full GBT buckling load within 0.3 %. Second, one network per lay-up takes the stringer pitch and the panel aspect ratio and returns an instant buckling load and eight candidate buckling modes. A design encoder (four layers of 256 neurons) feeds a decoder that builds the cross-section part of every length function, so the buckle wavelength and position can follow the design. It is trained on 152 designs by minimizing the GBT energy ratio (Rayleigh quotient) of its modes, alone or with up to 120 GBT results. Training only succeeded after the unknowns were scaled by the strain energy and every training round started with one GBT iteration step for each design. Third, the predicted modes start a short block eigenvalue iteration (LOBPCG) with the GBT matrices of the new design. Every step gives an upper bound of the buckling load in these unknowns, and the relative residual gives an error estimate without a reference solution; the iteration stops when it falls below a tolerance.

One network for all lay-ups kept maximum errors above 20 %, like standard data-driven models, hence one network per lay-up. For four lay-ups and two lateral boundary conditions (320 unseen designs), the networks trained with 120 GBT results predict the buckling load instantly within 4.9 %. Trained on physics alone, they overestimate the load but still provide starting modes. The error estimate was never smaller than the true error in 3800 checks. A 5 % tolerance is reached for every design without any GBT results, in 7 to 8 s instead of 11 s for full GBT; a Gaussian process needs 80 GBT results per lay-up and gives no guarantee. At the 22 Abaqus points of the earlier study, the new PINN with the iteration deviates from Abaqus by at most 1.19 %, compared with 10.80 % for the earlier PINN.
